# Trick Realism Plan

This plan focuses on making tricks feel more physical, readable, and believable without sacrificing the controller-first Flick-It input model.

The goal is not to add more trick names or more animation layers. The goal is to make the existing systems feel causally connected:

**preload → body prewind → takeoff impulse → compact body preserves rotation → grab reshapes the body → player opens up → rotation slows → head/chest spot the landing → skis align → landing absorbs the remaining error**

## Priorities

### P0 — Make rotation feel physically authored

#### 1. Make takeoff setup matter more

Current behavior gives most tricks a large angular impulse immediately when the Flick-It gesture commits. That is responsive, but it can make tricks feel like button-triggered rotations instead of movements generated by the skier.

Change the takeoff pipeline so setup depth, setup duration, gesture speed, and release direction contribute to stored rotational intent.

Target behavior:

- Holding the right stick down builds preload and body prewind.
- A shallow or rushed setup produces less rotation.
- A deep, deliberate setup produces more rotation.
- The stored rotation releases over a short takeoff window rather than in one frame.
- The visual prewind and the physical rotational impulse should describe the same action.
- A weak setup should naturally produce a 180 or underrotated 360 instead of receiving nearly full trick authority.

Likely files:

- `gameplay/trick_system/flick_trick_profile.gd`
- `gameplay/trick_system/flick_trick_interpreter.gd`
- `gameplay/trick_system/trick_command.gd`
- `player/skier_controller.gd`
- `player/animation/skier_animation_controller.gd`

Suggested implementation direction:

- Add explicit setup depth and setup duration to the trick command.
- Track stick travel velocity through the setup/release gesture.
- Calculate takeoff rotational energy from setup quality rather than stick magnitude alone.
- Apply the resulting angular impulse across roughly 100–180 ms after takeoff.
- Reduce the current minimum effective command strength so mediocre gestures are visibly weaker.

Acceptance criteria:

- Weak, medium, and strong takeoff gestures produce clearly different rotation rates.
- A 360 does not require arbitrary repeated midair flicks when the takeoff is strong enough.
- The animation prewind direction matches the physical trick direction.
- Takeoff rotation remains deterministic at 30, 60, and 120 Hz.

---

#### 2. Make body shape control airborne rotation

Current landing assistance can increase angular damping close to the ground even when there is little visible explanation for why the skier slows down.

Replace as much hidden rotational slowdown as practical with body-driven rotation control.

Target behavior:

- Compact body positions preserve rotation.
- Strong tuck/grab poses can slightly reduce damping.
- Opening arms and extending legs increases damping.
- Landing anticipation visibly opens the skier and causes the rotation to slow.
- The player can understand why a spin accelerates, maintains speed, or slows by looking at the skier.

Likely files:

- `player/skier_controller.gd`
- `player/animation/skier_animation_frame.gd`
- `player/animation/skier_animation_controller.gd`
- `player/animation/skier_animation_profile.gd`
- `resources/animation/default_animation_profile.tres`

Suggested implementation direction:

- Add a presentation-derived or gameplay-owned `rotation_compactness` value.
- Derive it from jump posture, active grab/style pose, and landing-open state.
- Use compactness to scale air angular damping within conservative limits.
- Keep landing assist as a safety layer, but reduce its influence once visible body-driven control is working.

Acceptance criteria:

- A tucked spin retains rotation longer than an opened spin.
- Opening before landing visibly and physically slows rotation.
- The same input does not produce unexplained last-second braking.
- Landing assist remains useful without looking automatic.

---

#### 3. Use more realistic trick axes

Current spin, flip, and cork commands are mostly fixed-axis impulses. Real ski tricks usually contain small coupled rotations caused by takeoff edge, body position, and approach direction.

Target behavior:

- Spins remain predominantly yaw but can carry subtle roll/pitch from the takeoff.
- Flips can inherit a small yaw component from approach and takeoff asymmetry.
- Corks use a continuously varying diagonal axis instead of one fixed yaw/roll ratio.
- Mirrored tricks remain physically and visually symmetric where appropriate.

Likely files:

- `gameplay/trick_system/flick_trick_interpreter.gd`
- `gameplay/trick_system/flick_trick_profile.gd`
- `player/skier_controller.gd`

Suggested implementation direction:

- Preserve more of the actual right-stick release vector.
- Map diagonal gesture angle continuously into cork yaw/roll contribution.
- Feed takeoff edge, steering direction, and switch stance into a small secondary-axis bias.
- Clamp all secondary-axis contributions so the control scheme remains predictable.

Acceptance criteria:

- Cork axes visibly vary with gesture direction.
- Spins no longer look perfectly turntable-flat every time.
- Extra axis motion remains subtle enough that players can still predict the result.

### P1 — Reduce motorized midair control

#### 4. Make takeoff responsible for most rotation

Repeated airborne flicks currently add substantial angular impulses. This is responsive, but it can make rotation feel powered rather than ballistic.

Target behavior:

- Takeoff provides roughly 75–90% of the rotational authority for a normal trick.
- Airborne flicks act as smaller continuation or correction inputs.
- Repeated flicking should not manufacture unlimited rotation.
- Strong midair correction should still be possible for accessibility and game feel, but should be visibly limited.

Likely files:

- `gameplay/trick_system/flick_trick_profile.gd`
- `gameplay/trick_system/flick_trick_interpreter.gd`
- `player/skier_controller.gd`

Suggested implementation direction:

- Separate takeoff impulse values from airborne continuation impulse values.
- Add diminishing returns to repeated in-air trick commits.
- Scale continuation authority by remaining airtime and current angular speed.
- Preserve the left-stick low-authority trim system for precise correction.

Acceptance criteria:

- Mashing repeated flicks cannot double or triple a poorly initiated spin.
- A strong takeoff still allows high-rotation tricks.
- Midair corrections remain useful for lining up landings.

---

#### 5. Integrate angular motion with quaternions

The current airborne rotation path applies local X, Y, and Z rotations sequentially each frame. Combined-axis tricks can inherit axis-order artifacts.

Target behavior:

- Angular velocity is integrated as one 3D rotational quantity.
- Corks and mixed-axis tricks remain stable through inversion.
- Orientation remains orthonormal without repeated axis-order bias.

Likely files:

- `player/skier_controller.gd`

Suggested implementation direction:

- Convert the local angular-velocity vector into an incremental axis-angle quaternion each physics frame.
- Multiply the skier basis/orientation by the incremental quaternion.
- Continue storing angular velocity in the skier's local/body frame if that remains the cleanest control representation.

Acceptance criteria:

- Existing spin and flip tests continue to pass.
- Corks remain smooth through inverted orientations.
- 30/60/120 Hz results remain within existing continuity tolerances.

### P1 — Improve the visual body mechanics

#### 6. Strengthen delayed body motion

The skier should not look like one rigid object rotating around the gameplay root.

Target behavior for spins:

1. shoulders/chest initiate,
2. pelvis follows,
3. skis and lower body lag slightly,
4. the body compacts through the middle of the rotation,
5. head/chest spot first,
6. arms open,
7. pelvis and skis finish the remaining rotation.

Target behavior for flips and corks should use the same principle with family-specific shapes.

Likely files:

- `player/animation/skier_animation_controller.gd`
- `player/animation/skier_animation_profile.gd`
- `resources/animation/default_animation_profile.tres`

Acceptance criteria:

- A 360 has visible initiation, rotation, spotting, and finish phases.
- Shoulders, pelvis, skis, hands, and poles do not all change direction simultaneously.
- Higher rotations remain readable rather than collapsing into a generic tucked pose.

---

#### 7. Add controlled asymmetry

Perfectly mirrored airborne poses look procedural quickly.

Target behavior:

- Small left/right ski-height differences.
- Slightly different knee compression.
- Mild pelvis yaw/offset.
- Unequal hand carriage.
- Directional pole lag.
- Asymmetry should come from takeoff edge, trick direction, switch stance, and existing deterministic style-side selection rather than random noise.

Likely files:

- `player/animation/skier_animation_controller.gd`
- `player/animation/skier_animation_profile.gd`

Acceptance criteria:

- Straight airs and spins no longer settle into perfectly mirrored silhouettes.
- Repeating the same deterministic setup remains stable for tests and captures.
- Left/right mirrored tricks still mirror correctly at the macro level.

---

#### 8. Make grabs move the ski toward the hand

A realistic-looking grab is a whole-body action, not just an arm reaching toward a marker.

Target sequence:

1. target knee compresses,
2. target ski rises or shifts,
3. pelvis moves toward the target,
4. torso counterbalances,
5. free arm changes the silhouette,
6. reaching arm finishes the contact.

This should also reduce the visual impact of the production body's shorter arm reach.

Likely files:

- `player/animation/skier_animation_controller.gd`
- `resources/animation/default_grab_animation_library.tres`
- `resources/animation/default_style_pose_library.tres`

Acceptance criteria:

- Grabs remain readable even when the wrist does not exactly reach the marker.
- The target ski clearly participates in the movement.
- Cross-body grabs have visibly different pelvis/torso mechanics from safety grabs.
- Existing grab-contact qualification remains gameplay-authoritative.

---

### P1 — Make landing quality visible and mechanically consistent

#### 9. Preserve underrotation and overrotation in the landing pose

Landing animation should not visually erase the error that the physics system measured.

Target behavior:

- Underrotated spins touch down with remaining body twist and recover through the knees/hips.
- Overrotated spins counter-rotate the chest and arms while the skis finish.
- Sketchy landings use asymmetric loading and wobble.
- Hard landings compress deeper and take longer to recover.
- Clean landings become visibly quieter and more centered.

Likely files:

- `player/skier_controller.gd`
- `player/animation/skier_animation_frame.gd`
- `player/animation/skier_animation_controller.gd`

Acceptance criteria:

- Clean, sketchy, and hard landings are visually distinguishable without HUD text.
- Opposite signed rotation residuals produce opposite recovery shapes.
- Landing animation never changes the gameplay transform to fake a correction.

---

#### 10. Tighten trick completion and naming around actual residual

Current trick naming rounds accumulated motion into coarse buckets. A visibly underrotated trick can still be credited as a completed rotation.

Target behavior:

- Compare actual accumulated rotation against the nearest intended 180° bucket.
- Track signed residual at landing.
- Use residual as part of landing quality and trick qualification.
- HUD/trick naming should agree with what the player sees.

Suggested starting bands for tuning:

- Clean: roughly within ±20–30° of the intended rotation.
- Sketchy: roughly ±30–55°.
- Larger residual: downgrade the completed rotation, heavily penalize landing quality, or bail depending on impact/upright state.

These numbers are tuning starting points, not final constants.

Likely files:

- `gameplay/trick_system/trick_controller.gd`
- `player/skier_controller.gd`
- `player/landing_solver.gd` or the current landing-quality path
- trick/landing acceptance tests

Acceptance criteria:

- A 140° turn does not present as a clean 180.
- A near-perfect 360 is credited consistently.
- Underrotation and overrotation produce signed, testable residuals.
- Trick text, score, landing quality, and animation all use the same authoritative rotation history.

## P2 — Camera and feedback polish

These should follow the physics/animation work so presentation does not hide mechanical problems.

### Camera

- Reduce camera reactions that exaggerate angular speed during tricks.
- Keep the skier readable during inversion without aggressively auto-leveling the apparent trick.
- Allow a small amount of delayed camera follow during spins so the body rotation remains visible against the environment.
- Make landing camera recovery follow the actual landing result rather than snapping immediately to neutral.

Likely files:

- `player/camera/` or current ski camera controller files

### Audio and rumble

- Use takeoff setup strength to scale pop feedback.
- Use rotation speed only subtly; avoid synthetic effects that imply a powered spin.
- Increase landing feedback distinction between clean, sketchy, hard, and bail outcomes.
- Keep rumble tied to physical events rather than animation phases.

Likely files:

- `autoload/audio_manager.gd`
- `autoload/input_manager.gd`

## Test plan

Every trick-realism change should extend deterministic coverage instead of relying only on subjective playtesting.

### Automated cases

Add or extend tests for:

- weak / medium / strong takeoff setup producing ordered angular velocity,
- setup duration and release speed affecting rotation predictably,
- 180 / 360 / 540 spin residuals,
- underrotated and overrotated landing classification,
- body-open versus body-compact angular damping,
- diminishing airborne continuation impulses,
- mirrored left/right behavior,
- continuous cork-axis mapping,
- quaternion integration equivalence across 30/60/120 Hz,
- grab compactness changing body shape without changing trajectory,
- landing animation preserving signed residual,
- root-authority invariants for all presentation layers.

Likely test files:

- `tests/flick_trick_acceptance.gd`
- `tests/flick_gameplay_acceptance.gd`
- `tests/trick_animation_acceptance.gd`
- `tests/grab_animation_acceptance.gd`
- `tests/landing_animation_acceptance.gd`
- `tests/animation_polish_acceptance.gd`
- `tests/physics_benchmark.gd`

### Human playtest checklist

After automated gates pass, test with a physical controller:

- Can a player intentionally produce weak versus strong spins?
- Does a good 360 feel earned at takeoff rather than corrected in the air?
- Do corks look diagonal rather than like yaw plus roll pasted together?
- Does opening the body visibly slow rotation?
- Can the player spot the landing through the skier's pose before touchdown?
- Do sketchy landings look sketchy before the HUD reports them?
- Do grabs read as whole-body movements?
- Can repeated airborne flicks still be abused to manufacture rotation?
- Does the camera preserve orientation and speed cues during high rotations?
- Are left/right and switch variants equally readable?

## Recommended implementation order

1. **Takeoff setup → stored rotation → short release window.**
2. **Body compactness → angular damping.**
3. **Signed rotation residual → stricter landing/trick qualification.**
4. **Reduced/diminishing airborne trick impulses.**
5. **Quaternion angular integration.**
6. **Continuous cork axes and subtle takeoff-axis coupling.**
7. **Delayed torso/pelvis/ski animation response.**
8. **Deterministic asymmetry.**
9. **Whole-body grab shaping.**
10. **Landing-error presentation, camera, audio, and rumble polish.**

The first three items should produce the largest improvement in perceived realism because they connect input, physics, animation, and landing outcome into one coherent system.

## Non-goals

This plan does not require:

- ragdoll tricks,
- a second gameplay state machine,
- root motion owning skier physics,
- replacing the existing Flick-It control scheme,
- an `AnimationTree` becoming authoritative over gameplay,
- exact hand-to-ski IK at the cost of silhouette quality,
- removing accessibility-oriented landing assistance entirely.

The existing ownership boundary should remain: gameplay owns trajectory, collision, and authoritative rotation; animation explains and reinforces those mechanics.
